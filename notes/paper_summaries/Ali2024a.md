## Research Objective
To provide a comprehensive review of Artificial Intelligence (AI) applications in cybersecurity specifically for smart agriculture (SA). The survey aims to cover the state-of-the-art of SA, conduct an in-depth literature study on cyber threats and challenges facing SA, investigate and synthesize AI approaches in cybersecurity for SA, explain how AI enhances cybersecurity in SA, and examine the ethical implications of employing AI in this context.

## Problem Addressed
Despite the revolutionary potential of smart agriculture (SA) through wireless sensor networks and IoT, the industry has become a frequent target for cyberattacks. Traditional cybersecurity techniques (preset rules, signatures) are static and unresponsive to new cyberattacks, making them inadequate for the dynamic threat landscape of SA. There is a lack of comprehensive reviews specifically describing AI techniques for improving cybersecurity in SA.

## Methodology
The study employs a **comprehensive literature review** approach:
*   **Literature Search**: Relevant literature was gathered from major academic databases and digital libraries (Nature, Wiley Online Library, MDPI, ScienceDirect, Frontiers, IEEE Xplore Digital Library, IGI Global, Springer, Taylor & Francis, and Google Scholar).
*   **Inclusion Criteria**: Papers published between January 2021 and July 2024, written in English, focusing on scientific and technical research in AI, cybersecurity, and SA.
*   **Keywords**: Used a combination of keywords and Boolean operators (e.g., "Cybersecurity Challenges in SA" OR "AI" AND "Cybersecurity" AND "SA").
*   **Selection**: Out of 320 publications, 180 research papers were ultimately chosen for detailed review.
*   **Data Extraction**: Predefined key information extracted included Title, authors, publication year, objectives, AI techniques used, cybersecurity applications, SA applications, and results/conclusions.
*   **Synthesis**: Qualitative synthesis and thematic analysis focused on categorizing AI techniques, identifying common cyber threats, and assessing the effectiveness of AI solutions in SA contexts.

## Results
*   **Evolution of Agriculture**: From 1.0 (Pre-Industrial) to 5.0 (Sustainable and Smart Agriculture), driven by technology (IoT, AI, blockchain, etc.). Agriculture 5.0 emphasizes sustainability, efficiency, and climate resilience.
*   **Smart Agriculture Architecture**: Comprises eight layers: Perception/Sensing, Networking/Data Communication, Edge, Fog, Cloud, Analytics, Control, and Application.
*   **Emerging Technologies in SA**: Detailed overview of WSNs, IoT, Smart Sensors, Agricultural Robotics, Smart Agricultural Drones, Satellite Imaging, GIS/Mapping, GPS, 5G communication, Cloud Computing, Blockchain technology, Digital Twins, Big Data Analytics, Additive Manufacturing, Agricultural Biotechnology/Genetic Engineering, Renewable Energy Solutions, and Smart Agriculture Applications.
*   **Applications of SA**: A comprehensive list of 17 applications: Monitoring climate conditions, Environment and field monitoring, Crop health monitoring, Precision agriculture, Livestock and poultry monitoring and management, Greenhouse automation, Smart irrigation systems, Crop monitoring, Pest and disease management, Fertilizer management, Intelligent agricultural machine, Smart harvesting, Soil management, Crop yield prediction, Weed management, Water management, Agricultural product quality and safety traceability.
*   **Cyber Threats and Challenges in SA (29 identified)**:
    1.  Data privacy concerns (e.g., John Deere's data policy, Farmobile lawsuit).
    2.  Data breaches and leakages (e.g., John Deere software vulnerabilities, Climate Corporation data).
    3.  Malware injection (ransomware, botnets; e.g., Agromart Group, JBS Foods).
    4.  Social engineering attacks (e.g., phishing).
    5.  DoS and DDoS attacks (e.g., Maple Leaf Foods outage).
    6.  Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) attacks (e.g., tractor telemetry, irrigation sensor data manipulation).
    7.  Replay attacks (e.g., GPS signals misguiding tractors).
    8.  Eavesdropping attacks (e.g., drone interception, water treatment facility).
    9.  Insider attacks (e.g., pest control system manipulation).
    10. Supply chain attacks (e.g., JBS ransomware, seed distributor cyberattack).
    11. Side-channel attacks (e.g., power monitoring soil sensors).
    12. Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs).
    13. Radio-frequency jamming attacks (e.g., GPS jamming at pig farm).
    14. Rogue device deployment.
    15. Sensing device capture attack/Node tampering (e.g., greenhouse sensor manipulation).
    16. Spoofing attack (e.g., GPS spoofing autonomous tractors).
    17. Agroterrorism (e.g., JBS ransomware, Ukraine critical infrastructure).
    18. False data injection attack (e.g., soil moisture sensors manipulating irrigation).
    19. Signature wrapping attacks.
    20. Reconnaissance attacks.
    21. SQL injection attacks (e.g., Agrisync platform).
    22. IoT breaches (e.g., ransomware on precision farms).
    23. Malicious hardware injection (e.g., US Dept of Homeland Security research).
    24. Autonomous system hijacking and disruption (e.g., Israeli water management system).
    25. Unauthorized Access (e.g., feed milling firm).
    26. Backdoor attack (e.g., Purdue University research).
    27. Sybil attacks.
    28. Black-hole attack.
    29. Cloud computing attacks, Cloud data leakage, Physical attack, Artificial intelligence attacks (evasion, data poisoning), Blockchain attacks, Tracing attack, Protocol attacks, Measure infusion forgery, Buffer overflow, RFID-based attacks, Account hijacking, Routing attacks.
*   **AI in SA Cybersecurity**: AI (ML, DL, NLP, RL) is essential for automated threat prediction, endpoint protection, botnet detection, early/faster detection of new threats, fast response, higher accuracy, lower cost, and improved decision-making. AI market value in cybersecurity expected to rise from US$8.8 billion in 2020 to US$38.2 billion by 2026.
*   **Ethical Concerns**: Data privacy, requirement for high-quality information, complexities of AI implementation.

## Limitations
*   As a literature review, it identifies potential limitations in its own methodology: possibility of missing relevant studies, publication bias, not thoroughly investigating ethical implications, lack of quantitative analysis, insufficient emphasis on real-world implementation, and rapid advancements in AI/cybersecurity outpacing literature.
